00 / Statement Artist Statement

A collection of fifteen geometric narratives — life, seen through shapes and stories.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of geometric abstraction and narrative. My work begins with a simple observation: every life is a composition of small recurring forms — circles, rectangles, triangles — arranged by attention and by time. To draw those forms is to notice the shape of a life.

Symphony of Life is a collection of fifteen works, each drawn with geometric primitives in p5.js and accompanied by a written story. The works move between genres: urban observation (crooked buildings, a dance studio), botanical reflection (a tree's silhouette, Maya and the oak), speculative narrative (a fish named Azure, a blood cell named Remy), and controlled abstraction (Matrix Rain 01, The Hextech Singularity, VIOLET STRATA).

Every work carries its own story — the story is part of the work, not a caption. Together the fifteen pieces form a small library of fables, each told in the same geometric language but with a different voice.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

01 / Primitives

Geometric Vocabulary

Every work in this collection is drawn from a small set of geometric primitives: circles, rectangles, triangles, quads, bézier curves, and lines. Every shape is placed by code, so the work is legible at any scale — from a print to a wall. No raster image, no imported texture, no photograph.

02 / Story

Each Work Has One

Every work carries a written story — a short prose piece that accompanies the composition. The stories range from whimsical (an inventor obsessed with hexagons) to melancholic (a fish who longs for the world above the water). The story is part of the work, not a caption.

03 / Life

A Symphony of Forms

The collection is held together by the idea of life — the small recurring forms that shape a life, the stories that give those forms meaning. Some works literally depict living things (trees, fish, owls, blood cells); others are about the shapes life takes as it moves through time.
